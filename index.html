How it works

Owning a US LLC from abroad is easy to start and easy to neglect. The company keeps existing after the formation, and with it come yearly duties: a registered agent in its state, a state annual report or fee, and a federal filing with the IRS.

For a single-member LLC owned by a non-US person, the IRS expects Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, even when the LLC had no income. A missed or incomplete Form 5472 carries an IRS penalty that runs into the tens of thousands of dollars.

The state side is separate: depending on the state where the LLC was formed, there is a yearly report or fee with its own deadline. Notices are sent to the registered agent's address, which many owners never see.

The service handles all three in one yearly plan. It acts as or arranges the registered agent for your LLC in its state, and every letter that arrives there is scanned and sent to you by email with a short note on what it means.

Before the state deadline, the annual report is prepared from the data on file and sent to you for a quick check. After you confirm, it is filed with the state, and you receive the confirmation. State fees are paid at cost and shown separately.

For the federal side, you answer a short questionnaire about the money that moved between you and the LLC during the year. Form 5472 and the pro forma Form 1120 are prepared, you review them, and they are filed with the IRS.

All deadlines for your LLC — state and federal — appear in one calendar, with reminders by email well before each one. Every filed document, confirmation and scanned letter is kept in one place, so you can show your LLC's history to a bank or a payment provider when they ask.

The plan is built for LLCs with no business activity in the US beyond holding an account and receiving payments. If your LLC has employees, US offices, several owners or a change of ownership, the questionnaire tells you before you pay that the simple plan does not fit.

The service is built for non-US freelancers, online sellers, developers and consultants who own a single-member US LLC. It is in preparation: we are checking whether LLC owners need it before it is built.
